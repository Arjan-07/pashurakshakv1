PASHURAKSHAK — POTENTIAL CLUSTER GUIDE
=======================================

Purpose
-------
A Potential Cluster is an early-warning signal. It does not diagnose a disease.
It helps veterinary teams notice when several recent animal-health reports are
close to one another and have enough combined risk to deserve field review.

How it is created
------------------
1. A farmer submits a health report with an animal, symptoms, affected-animal
   count and village/area.
2. The system records the report location.
3. The system checks recent reports from the last 48 hours.
4. If at least 2 recent reports are geographically close (about 0.15 degrees)
   and the combined surveillance score reaches the cluster threshold, a
   Potential Cluster is created or updated.
5. The Risk Zones screen groups signals into:
      GREEN  0–30   Routine monitoring
      YELLOW 31–60   Veterinary review
      RED    61–100  Priority field action

Simple example
--------------
Farmer A reports fever and reduced appetite in a cattle animal in Village X.
Farmer B reports similar signs in another animal in the same nearby area.
The reports are recent and geographically close. Their combined signal can
create a Potential Cluster.

What the veterinarian should do
--------------------------------
1. Open Dashboard.
2. Open Risk zones.
3. Check the Green / Yellow / Red cards and the village-level map.
4. Open the Potential Cluster card and review:
   - number of reports
   - affected animals
   - nearby villages
   - risk score
   - current field-assessment status
5. If field verification is required, click Assign assessment.
6. Choose the field worker.
7. Add practical instructions, for example:
   "Visit the affected villages, verify symptoms, check vaccination records,
    and collect samples if indicated by the veterinary protocol."
8. After assignment, open Investigations to track the field workflow.
9. Collect a sample when required. The investigation moves into laboratory
   testing.
10. In Laboratory, the veterinarian can record the laboratory result.
11. When field action is complete, the field worker can mark the investigation
    resolved.

How to make a cluster appear while testing
-------------------------------------------
Use two or more health reports that:
- are submitted within the recent 48-hour window;
- use the same or nearby village/area;
- contain meaningful symptoms;
- affect one or more animals.

For a quick test, submit two reports from the same area using symptoms such as
fever, lethargy or reduced appetite. The application uses the entered report
location, so keep the village/area consistent while testing.

Why a cluster may not appear
-----------------------------
- Only one recent report exists.
- Reports are too far apart.
- Reports are older than the recent surveillance window.
- The combined surveillance score is below the cluster threshold.
- The browser/app was not refreshed after a report was submitted.

If this happens
---------------
1. Submit another nearby report.
2. Open Risk zones again.
3. Use Refresh map.
4. Check the Potential Cluster count.
5. If still absent, confirm that the report was successfully submitted in My
   Reports / Reports & review.

How to read the map
-------------------
- The OpenStreetMap basemap shows real geographic locations.
- Report/cluster circles are coloured by surveillance band.
- Village names entered in reports are geocoded through OpenStreetMap's
  Nominatim service when available.
- If a village boundary geometry is returned, it is drawn on the map.
- Map colours are semantic risk colours only; they are not the site's brand
  palette.

Important safety note
---------------------
Potential Cluster and Risk Zone are surveillance aids. They are not a disease
confirmation, diagnosis, or treatment recommendation. Veterinary professionals
should follow authorised diagnostic, sampling, biosecurity and reporting SOPs.

Data / service notes
--------------------
- The livestock catalogue is exposed by the local free endpoint:
      GET /api/livestock/catalog
  It contains India-relevant livestock species and common breeds and works
  without a paid database service.
- The disease/sample reference is exposed by:
      GET /api/knowledge/diseases
      GET /api/knowledge/samples
- The map uses OpenStreetMap tiles and Nominatim geocoding. Respect the public
  service usage policies and move to a hosted GIS/geocoder for high traffic.
